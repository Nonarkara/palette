# About Palette

Palette began with a simple dissatisfaction: important colour references are
usually encountered as thumbnails, grids, or shopping tools. Those formats make
comparison efficient, but they make colour small. This project gives each of
Sanzo Wada's combinations the scale of a room.

It is an independent, non-commercial interpretation made by Dr Non
Arkaraprasertkul. The exhibition does not reproduce the book's scans, page
design, cover, or publisher copy. Its source material is the credited,
MIT-licensed digital dataset described in
[`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## The research question

What can an old catalogue teach a contemporary interface when the colours are
allowed to become architecture rather than decoration?

Wada's combinations suggest that harmony is relational. A hue is not fixed in
experience: area, neighbour, lightness, and sequence change what the eye reads.
Palette therefore refuses the neutral swatch grid. One relationship fills one
viewport. Unequal areas make hierarchy visible. Grayscale reveals value
structure. Search treats names and computed qualities as ways into the archive,
not as replacements for looking.

## Dr Non's Digest

“Natural gastronomy” is the project's working metaphor. A pairing can be
balanced without being polite. Salt makes fruit sharper; bitterness gives sugar
an edge; a small acidic note can organize a whole dish. Colour combinations do
similar work through proportion and contrast. The best plates do not erase
disagreement. They give it a useful size.

The software's readings—temperature, energy, value interval, and possible
use—are original editorial metadata derived deterministically from the digital
values. They are invitations to inspect, not historical claims about Wada's
intent.

## The exhibition rule

The interface is museum hardware: clear enough to use, quiet enough to vanish.
The colour relationship owns the room. Typography, symbols, and motion exist to
move between objects and to disclose context. Nothing is rounded, elevated, or
decorated into importance.

The references are Wada's plates, Rothko's scale, Bauhaus geometry, and MoMA's
object-first hierarchy. They are a lineage, not a costume.

## Language and access

The interpretive text is written in English, Thai, and Simplified Chinese.
Each script receives an appropriate typeface. Controls have text alternatives,
keyboard paths, visible focus, and live state announcements. Colour is the
subject, but it is never the only way information is communicated.

The Mama Rule remains the release ceiling: if an older, nontechnical first-time
visitor cannot move, search, understand, and return without coaching, the room
is not finished.

## A personal interval

Dr Non's favourite pairing is red and black: classy and dangerous. Red carries
heat, appetite, and warning; black supplies discipline, depth, and a stopping
point. The interest lies in proportion—in the moment raw energy becomes poise—
not in treating either colour as a fixed symbol.

The site's About / Research room uses the catalogue's credited digital Red and
Black values as a personal study. It is deliberately labelled outside Wada's
348 combinations rather than invented as a historical plate.
