# About Palette

Colour books usually arrive as thumbnails, grids, or shops. Those formats make comparison quick, and they keep the colour small. Palette gives each of Sanzo Wada’s 348 combinations the size of a room.

Dr Non Arkaraprasertkul made it as an independent, non-commercial reading of that catalogue. It does not reproduce the book’s scans, page design, cover, or publisher’s sentences. The screen values come from the credited MIT dataset named in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). They are conversions for a display, not measurements of the printed ink.

## The question

What can an old catalogue teach a contemporary interface, once the colours are allowed to become the architecture instead of the decoration?

Wada’s combinations treat harmony as a relationship. A hue is not fixed in experience. Area, neighbour, lightness, and sequence change what the eye reads. This room therefore refuses the neutral swatch grid. One relationship fills one viewport. Unequal areas make the hierarchy visible. Grayscale removes hue so the value structure can be judged. Search is a way into the archive. It does not replace looking.

## Dr Non’s Digest

“Natural gastronomy” is the working metaphor. A pairing can be balanced without being polite. Salt makes fruit sharper. Bitterness gives sugar an edge. A small acid note can hold a whole plate of food together. Colour does similar work through proportion and contrast. The best combinations do not erase disagreement. They give it a useful size.

Temperature, energy, value, contrast, and the suggested room are editorial metadata computed from the digital values. The list of rooms is closed and lives in the program. The labels are invitations to look again. They are not historical claims about what Wada intended, and they do not call a model.

## The exhibition rule

The interface is museum hardware: clear enough to use, quiet enough to leave the colour alone. Typography, marks, and motion exist to move between objects and to say where the object came from. Nothing is rounded, lifted, or decorated into importance.

The references are Wada’s plates, Rothko’s scale, Bauhaus geometry, and the habit in MoMA’s collection interfaces of letting the object lead. They are a lineage. They are not a costume. The borrowed-idea list, including what was not copied, is [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md).

## Language

The Digest is written in English, Thai, and Simplified Chinese. Thai is set in a non-looped face with `lang="th"`. Chinese is Simplified, with `lang="zh-Hans"`. Controls have text names, keyboard paths, visible focus, and live announcements. Colour is the subject. It is never the only carrier.

The Mama Rule is still the ceiling for a finished room. If an older, nontechnical visitor cannot move, search, understand, and return without coaching, the room is not finished. That visit has not been run. The public edition stays `0.1.0` until it has.

## A personal interval

Dr Non’s own pairing is red and black: classy and dangerous. Red carries heat, appetite, and warning. Black supplies a stopping point. The interest is the proportion, the moment the heat becomes poise, not a fixed symbol for either colour.

The About room uses the catalogue’s credited digital Red `#A72144` and Black `#111314` for that study. It is labelled as personal. It is not one of Wada’s 348 combinations.
